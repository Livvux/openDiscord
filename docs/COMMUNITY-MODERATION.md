# Community rules and ban appeals

Community managers can publish up to 4,000 characters of plain-text participation rules in community settings. Rules are displayed as text, including any HTML-like input. These are technical community controls and operator-written participation rules; this document does not assert legal review or supply legal terms.

When acknowledgement is enabled, a new member must acknowledge the current version on a valid invite page. Invalid, expired, exhausted or revoked invites do not expose rules. Existing members can read the updated rules in their community and must acknowledge the current version before sending or editing messages or adding reactions. A stale acknowledgement cannot accept a later version. Disabling the requirement removes this sending restriction. The requirement also applies to integration member accounts; enable it only when that restriction is intended for automated posting.

A ban removes membership and community access. A signed-in person can open **Your community restrictions** (`/app/restrictions`) to see their own current bans and the reason supplied by moderators. Ban prompts disclose that the reason is visible to the banned person and in the community audit log. This page does not provide community messages, channels, member lists, moderator identities or other people's restrictions.

The banned person can submit one plain-text appeal per ban issuance, up to 2,000 characters. Only that person and current community members with **Ban members** permission can read it. Moderators review the latest 100 appeals in community settings and explicitly uphold or remove the ban, optionally providing a reply of up to 512 characters. An issuer's current rank limits both decisions, matching ordinary unban protections. Removing a ban does not restore membership; rejoining requires a valid invite. A new ban is a separate issuance and permits a new appeal. Ordinary unban also closes a pending appeal for the affected issuance.

Appeal submission and decisions create audit events without appeal content or moderator replies. Resolved decisions remain visible in the appellant's history after an unban; current bans keep their original reason. Community/account deletion cascades to their appeal records. Pending appeals survive maintenance; resolved appeals follow the 180-day maintenance retention window. The instance operator must schedule the maintenance command as described in the hosting documentation.

Automated coverage: `apps/web/tests/community-trust.integration.test.ts` exercises permission and community boundaries, exact versions, duplicate concurrent submissions, concurrent resolution and reissued bans. `apps/web/e2e/community-trust.spec.ts` covers authenticated join, updated rules, private appeal submission and moderator resolution on both desktop and mobile projects. Passing automation is separate from external legal review and a professional security audit.
